What makes the composition physical?

Each component stores energy H, exchanges it through ports, and dissipates some through R. The port output is paired with the input so their product is power.

ẋi = (Ji − Ri) ∇Hi + Biui

The internal connection redistributes power without creating it: ŷTû = 0. A line or contact that stores or dissipates energy needs its own physical account. This classical cancellation property constrains the model; it does not establish prediction accuracy or an exact finite-step balance.

1 of 4 · Simulated arms

Train with four joints.
What happens when there are eight?

Training4 jointsLearn from this arm only
Testing2–8 jointsReuse the local laws, no refitting

A model that treats the whole arm as one fixed-size object cannot simply accept twice as many joints. C-PHAST reuses a local dynamics model for each joint and assembles it at the new size.

Give it five position observations and the upcoming torques. Then stop supplying observations: can it predict the next one second of motion?

One second is 50 model updates. This controlled simulator tests repeated joint dynamics and their coupling, not a full industrial arm with arbitrary contacts.

The local laws can carry over. How well depends on what they know.

With supplied local physics, errors stay small at the tested sizes. When local physics must be learned, the longer forecasts are less accurate, even though the model still runs at the new count.

New coupling entries beyond the four-joint training block were not learned. This demonstrates reuse of local laws, not discovery of arbitrary new interactions.

3 of 4 · A decision in simulation

The robot has been pushed.
Which response would you try?

Keep the same body and connections. Now the choice is the command: continue walking, move right, return toward the default joint stance, or use another saved candidate.

A separate body-plus-legs model predicts the next 0.16 seconds. A trained selector chooses a command from those predictions and the current state. The simulator then executes that choice independently for 0.48 seconds, before resuming the normal command.

This is not the transferred leg model from the previous study. The detailed walkthrough uses recorded measurements, not a new simulation.

It delays failure on average.
It does not produce more recoveries than the best fixed response.

Across all 20 cases, the model-based choice gains 0.105 seconds in mean survival score over always moving right. Both recover in 10 of 20 cases; some model-based choices are worse.

That is a narrower finding than “the robot learns to recover.” The benefit is in comparing responses, and it is not uniform.

4 of 4 · Real Spot recordings

Can we estimate the work and battery draw
behind a real robot’s motion?

Watching the robot move does not tell us how much work its joints do or how much current it draws. Its recorded forces, motion and battery measurements let us check those quantities.

Fit consequence predictors using four recordings; compare their outputs with measurements from two held-out recordings.

This is a retrospective check. Some inputs describe motion that already happened, so these results do not show that the system could make the same prediction before acting.

The estimates follow measured work and current in held-out recordings.

When measured work or battery draw rises, the predictions tend to rise too. Correlation near 1 means strong agreement in variation; it does not mean the numerical error is zero.

This checks physical readouts from real data. It does not demonstrate a deployed C-PHAST controller or a complete learned-dynamics rollout on Spot.

What is measured, and what would live use still need?

Work integrates torque × joint velocity over time. Positive work counts positive contributions; absolute work counts their magnitudes. Battery draw here is mean discharge current, not battery energy. Training uses 897 windows from four bags; testing uses 173 from two held-out bags. Histories span two seconds and outcomes five seconds.

Inputs include descriptors measured from the executed future. Before live use, those descriptors would have to be supplied by a planner or forecasted. 03 / Reliable comparisons

Which futures can we actually distinguish?

A low predicted cost is not enough. Its error margin must be small enough to separate alternatives and test operating limits.

Shared uncertainty need not hide the difference.

Two candidates begin from the same uncertain state. Bounding their difference together preserves information that separate bounds can discard.

Mechanical specialization

$R_\theta$ is the dissipation operator on the full phase state. $D_\theta(q)$ is its mechanical damping block and is the quantity evaluated in the physical-recovery study.

$$R_\theta(q)=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\0&D_\theta(q)\end{bmatrix},\qquad D_\theta(q)\succeq0.$$

3 · Scaling and identification

Forecasting can improve while physical recovery fails.

We vary one experimental axis at a time and read two outcomes separately: long-horizon prediction error and recovery of the damping law.

A model can predict a trajectory for the wrong physical reason. In PHAST, an overly flexible damping channel may absorb errors in the potential or mass and still stabilize the rollout. The scaling study therefore asks not only whether the forecast improves, but also whether the learned damping becomes more faithful.

Model and measures

The same dynamics are judged in two different ways.

PHAST evolves a port-Hamiltonian state $x=(q,p)$ with stored energy $H_\theta$. The damping operator $R_\theta\succeq0$ removes energy; an external port can supply it.

$$H_\theta(q,p)=V_\theta(q)+\tfrac12p^\top M_\theta(q)^{-1}p,$$ $$\dot{x}=\bigl(J-R_\theta(x)\bigr)\nabla H_\theta(x)+G_\theta(x)u,$$ $$\dot H_\theta=-\nabla H_\theta^\top R_\theta\nabla H_\theta+u^\top y^{\mathrm{port}}.$$

Forecasting

$$\mathcal E_{\mathrm{roll}}(H)=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{test}}Hd_q}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{test}}}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\lVert\hat q_h^{(i)}-q_h^{(i)}\rVert_2^2$$

Lower is better. $N_{\mathrm{test}}$ is the number of test trajectories, $H$ the forecast horizon, and $d_q$ the number of observed coordinates. Angular differences are wrapped before the norm is evaluated.

Physical recovery

$$R_D^2=1-\frac{\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{test}}}\lVert D_\theta(q)-D^\star(q)\rVert_F^2}{\sum_{q\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{test}}}\lVert D^\star(q)-\bar D^\star\rVert_F^2}$$

Higher is better. $\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{test}}$ is the test configuration set, $D^\star$ the simulator damping law, and $\bar D^\star$ its mean. A negative score is worse than predicting that mean everywhere.

Observed data

$$B_{\mathrm{obs}}=N_{\mathrm{traj}}T_{\mathrm{traj}},$$ $$B_K=N_{\mathrm{traj}}(T_{\mathrm{traj}}-K+1).$$

$B_{\mathrm{obs}}$ counts observed position samples and $B_K$ counts overlapping $K$-step histories. The study varies $N_{\mathrm{traj}}\in\{64,256\}$ while holding $T_{\mathrm{traj}}=160$ fixed.

Not measured here: scaling with the number of samples per trajectory, $T_{\mathrm{traj}}$.

Identification result

Excitation reveals the damping law; a bound prevents pathological attribution.

At width 64, increasing the training set helps recovery only when the trajectories contain informative motion. Broad excitation raises the best test-set damping score to $R_D^2=0.437$. Under narrow excitation, the same model sees too little of the state space and reaches only $0.097$.

The uncapped PSD model produces strongly negative scores in every matched condition. Bounding damping removes this demonstrated error sink, but it does not prove that the full physical decomposition is uniquely identifiable.

How to read $R_D^2$

$1$ is exact recovery, $0$ is no better than predicting the mean damping law, and a negative value is worse than that constant predictor.

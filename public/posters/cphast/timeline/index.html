C-PHAST Advances and Dissertation Path

The project asks how a physical world model can remain trustworthy as a system changes, explain why candidate futures differ, and help decide both what the system should do and how it should reconfigure. This page separates demonstrated advances from the remaining dissertation tests.

Proposed thesis spine

Structure-preserving world models for reconfigurable physical systems

From partial observations and compositional transfer to closed-loop action and active physical reconfiguration.

  1. Q1RepresentationWhat physical object should be learned once and reused when subsystem count, topology, embodiment, or sensing changes?
  2. Q2Counterfactual predictionCan the model distinguish candidate interventions through named energy, work, support, sensing, and residual consequences?
  3. Q3Reconfiguration and controlCan the system choose both what to do and what physical configuration to become, while accounting for uncertainty and reconfiguration work?

Scientific progress

Five comparative milestones

Each period records the limitation that existed beforehand, the closest relevant comparator, the advance made, quantitative or theorem-level evidence, and the next obstruction exposed by the result.

  1. May 1-14Theory + architecture
    Q1vs PHAST, graph models, compositional PHNNs

    The reusable object became a typed physical block

    Starting limitation
    PHAST preserved pH structure but was fixed-layout. Graph models resized but did not constrain storage, dissipation, or exchanged power.
    Advance
    Shared typed pH blocks were separated from a deployment specification containing count, wiring, observations, charts, and metadata. Rollouts retained named physical consequences before scoring.
    Evidence
    For every learned skew interconnection, internal coupling contributes exactly zero net continuous-time power; PSD local damping prevents negative dissipation by construction.
    What remained
    The decomposition and target layout were still declared. New component types and unknown topology were outside scope.
    Composition contract
  2. May 19-23Real-robot forecasting
    Q2vs direct scalar score prediction

    Spot telemetry became physically typed future consequences

    Starting limitation
    Decision-facing world models commonly expose a latent, reward, or scalar value, leaving the physical reason for a ranking implicit.
    Advance
    A real-data query mapped recent Spot-with-arm telemetry and a numeric possible-next-behavior descriptor to work, support, battery, collision, sensing, and novelty consequences.
    Evidence
    On 173 held-out windows, energetic-target Pearson correlation was 0.943-0.975. Typed-then-scored objectives achieved mean Pearson 0.880 and MAE 0.274, versus 0.871 and 0.330 for direct scalar training.
    What remained
    Six logged episodes support forecasting, not autonomous hardware action or matched-state alternative futures.
    Spot evidence
  3. May 28-Jun 18Comparative validation
    Q1vs Shared-GRU, MPNN, DeepSets, N-PHDAE

    Redeployment became a falsifiable benchmark contract

    Starting limitation
    Variable-size support alone did not distinguish reusable physics from parameter sharing, and specialized electrical models remained stronger in their native regime.
    Advance
    Matched information contracts separated changes in subsystem count, embodiment, topology, and observation interface across mechanics, excitable systems, and microgrids.
    Evidence
    After training at four legs, C-PHAST reached H=50 MSE 0.002/0.005/0.003 at two/four/six legs, versus 0.013/0.014/0.020 for Shared-GRU; MPNN and DeepSets were unstable.
    What remained
    N-PHDAE remained the stronger full-state known-graph electrical forecaster. C-PHAST's differentiator was redeployment and typed diagnostics, not a blanket raw-MSE win.
    Benchmark contracts
  4. Jul 7-Aug 1Guarantees + transfer
    Q1vs monolithic PHAST and matched sequence models

    The transfer claim acquired both a bound and a canonical test

    Starting limitation
    Continuous-time pH structure did not by itself quantify finite-step accounting, observer error, or cross-robot degradation.
    Advance
    Added a third-order finite-step ledger defect under the stated quadrature assumptions and an observer-aware transfer bound separating local, edge, port, and numerical error.
    Evidence
    On canonical ANYmal-D to Go2 takeover, C-PHAST obtains 0.073 MSE versus 0.145 for monolithic PHAST and 0.178 for Shared-GRU. Its transfer gap is 1.6x versus 3.7x, 13x, and 21x for relevant alternatives.
    What remained
    Hybrid contacts, learned modules, q-only observer error, and configuration-dependent inertia remain outside the strongest certificate.
    Canonical transfer evidence
  5. Aug 8-10Executed decision
    Q2 -> Q3vs fixed recovery and pH-readout ablation

    Predicted consequences changed an executed command

    Starting limitation
    Spot was shadow mode and the push critic behaved primarily as a warning system; neither established that forecasts altered execution.
    Advance
    A bounded selector used eight-step C-PHAST consequences to choose one of nine high-level commands, then a frozen locomotion policy executed the choice for 24 steps.
    Evidence
    Against the best fixed command, mean survival rose from 116.55 to 121.80 steps: +5.25 with seed-clustered 95% CI [2.5, 7.05]. pH readouts improved longest-survival selection from 12/20 to 14/20.
    What remained
    Binary recovery tied at 10/20, selection occurred only once, and action-direction sensitivity remained weaker than required for a general controller.
    Executed-selection evidence

Frontier definition

What would put C-PHAST at the top of the mountain?

Three problems now dominate. Each is stated as a research claim with a measurable completion gate, not as an open-ended feature list.

Main research goal

Can a compositional physical world model remain trustworthy as a system changes, and use physical forecasts to decide how it should act or reconfigure next?

The program separates three causal questions: infer a configuration change that happened to the system, choose an action while configuration is fixed, and deliberately change configuration as part of the action. Calibrated uncertainty determines when any of these decisions should instead request another observation or abstain.

Established operational problem

A physical grid can change before its operational model does

Power-systems literature calls this topology-error processing, switch-status identification, or operational topology identification. A breaker or sectionalizing switch may change the energized feeder while its status is missing, delayed, incorrectly transmitted, or still awaiting a manual model update. The resulting stale model can corrupt state estimation, forecasting, restoration, and control decisions.

  1. Distribution Network Topology Identification Using Asynchronous Transformer Monitoring DataAligns the management-system topology with the physical feeder after switching for fault isolation or reconfiguration.
  2. WLAV State Estimation Based Topology Error Detection and IdentificationAddresses missing or incorrectly transferred switch status under limited distribution-grid measurements.
  3. Detection of Topology Errors by State EstimationEstablishes the classical residual-based problem caused by erroneous breaker and switch telemetry.
  4. Simultaneous Robust State Estimation, Topology Error Processing, and Outage DetectionJointly handles state, topology, and outage uncertainty and evaluates the method on an actual distribution feeder.
  5. Learning Distribution Grid Topologies: A TutorialSurveys topology learning from sparse grid measurements and its role in situational awareness.

C-PHAST frontier: prior work establishes the need to identify the energized topology. Our question goes further: can typed dynamic residuals identify which physical relation changed, synchronize the same compositional twin without retraining its local blocks, and immediately restore trustworthy counterfactual forecasts?

  6. 06

    Each candidate topology produces a telemetry prediction and a residual. The existing ring-rewire experiment amplifies energy, line-current, and interconnect-power residuals by 37×, 7.0×, and 5.9×.

    The residual signal is demonstrated; automatic identification of the correct change is not.

  7. 07
    Proposed frontier · structural inference The extension infers what changed

    Typed rollout residuals score candidate feeder, component, parameter, and sensor-change hypotheses. The previous mode supplies temporal persistence.

    If the posterior remains diffuse or every candidate fits poorly, C-PHAST abstains instead of silently changing its structure.

  8. 08
    Proposed frontier · synchronization The same twin synchronizes and resumes forecasting

    C-PHAST selects the inferred topology mode, replaces only its deployed incidence matrix, refreshes its state estimate, and keeps the learned DGU and line dynamics fixed.

Evidence boundary: mode-conditioned forecasting and typed residuals are demonstrated; automatic inference of and online synchronization are the proposed frontier.
Internal inference · Frontier 01 Synchronize the twin

Infer the topology mode, update the incidence matrix, refresh the physical state, and forecast. No breaker or converter command is issued.

Physical action · Frontier 02 Operate the grid

A supervisory controller may redispatch converters, close a healthy tie switch, shed load, form an island, or defer to an operator using C-PHAST forecasts.

Important: if a planned switch command or reliable breaker-status message already reports the new topology, C-PHAST receives that mode directly. Structural diagnosis is needed only for an unreported, delayed, uncertain, or fault-driven change.

Closed-loop experiment · one cycle at a time

What enters C-PHAST, what it returns, and who acts

The model predicts candidate consequences. A separate planner selects a high-level command, and the frozen locomotion policy converts that command into joint actions.

  1. 01
    Current and proposed · observation Measure the disturbed robot

    After the lateral push, C-PHAST receives recent root and joint telemetry, contact/slip geometry, declared terrain material, and the supplied command library. It receives no push label, push magnitude, or future simulator state.

  2. 02
    Current and proposed · matched choices Start every candidate from the same inferred state

    The nine labels are nominal, hold, forward, backward, left, right, turn-left, turn-right, and default stance. Candidate access is identical across predictive models.

  3. 03
    Current and proposed · action-conditioned rollout Forecast the physical future under every command

    The submitted one-shot study uses an eight-step forecast. The repeated experiment will sweep the useful lookahead instead of assuming that a longer horizon is always better.

  4. 04
    Current and proposed · fixed downstream planner Convert each rollout into decision-facing consequences

    The consequence vector contains predicted stability, support/contact, slip, energy and dissipation, task progress, and other declared factors. The planner, not C-PHAST, specifies how those consequences are prioritized.

  5. 05
    Current and proposed · physical execution Execute the selected high-level command briefly

    The frozen RSL-RL policy maps the selected velocity command to low-level joint actions. In the current study, the choice is executed for 24 control steps and no second planning decision is made.

  6. 06
    Proposed frontier · close the loop Refresh the observer and make the next decision

    After a short execution interval, new telemetry replaces the old belief. Forecasts are recomputed from the realized state rather than chaining indefinitely through model predictions.

Frontier scientific problem · active physical reconfiguration

Control must choose both the next state and the dynamics that produce it

Ordinary action moves a system inside its current configuration. Reconfiguration changes shape, stiffness, contact, active components, or connectivity, and therefore changes the model needed for the next forecast.

Question 01Change happened to the system

Damage, sensor loss, or an unreported contact change alters configuration. C-PHAST must infer what changed.

Question 02Action changes ordinary state

A command moves the system while its declared morphology, sensors, and connectivity remain fixed.

Question 03Action changes configuration

The system deliberately changes shape, contacts, active modules, sensing, or connectivity to complete the task.

Why this is a cross-domain control problem

The same decision appears whenever configuration changes the governing dynamics

Continuous reconfiguration is needed when useful configurations form a continuum and intermediate shapes affect safety, work, or performance. Hybrid reconfiguration combines those smooth adjustments with discrete contact, topology, or module transitions.

Continuous Shape, stiffness, geometry

The configuration evolves smoothly while the task continues. Intermediate configurations are physical states, not free jumps.

Discrete Contact, topology, active modules

Do not collapse every error into one variance

Three uncertainties require different evidence

Physical randomness, partial observation, and an incorrect deployment model are not interchangeable. The risk layer should expose their source before it decides whether more sensing or a conservative action can help.

State beliefWhat is the robot doing now?

Partial and delayed telemetry leave uncertainty in velocity, momentum, contact, and other hidden state. Anchored filtering updates this belief when measurements arrive.

Resolved by informative observations when the observer is valid.
Physical processHow variable is the future?

Contact fluctuations, sensor noise, and stochastic forcing generate different outcomes even under the same state and command.

Represented by process noise; stochastic PHAST constrains its dissipative placement.
Model / deploymentIs the twin itself wrong?

Changed payload, friction, contact law, sensing, or embodiment can invalidate learned dynamics or calibration.

Requires held-out residual calibration, ensembles, or change detection; FDT noise alone cannot represent it.

Stochastic PHAST already identifies three design constraints

Noise follows dissipation

The stochastic pH model uses fluctuation–dissipation collocation, so physical process noise enters through declared dissipative channels rather than an unconstrained output variance.

The energy ledger calibrates

The stochastic energy ledger estimates a noise floor and audits generator consistency. It cannot detect plant divergence when evaluated only on the model's own rollout.

Observation timing needs guardrails

Unconstrained score triggers can measure repeatedly and then leave long gaps. Fixed or deadline-clamped cadence is the robust baseline before state-dependent re-observation is trusted.

Integration frontier: carry this belief and noise accounting through the compositional C-PHAST rollout, then add separately calibrated model/deployment uncertainty before using it for hardware decisions.

Risk-aware decision · one cycle in order

From telemetry to execute, re-observe, or abstain

C-PHAST supplies distributions over typed consequences. The risk selector applies the declared objective and confidence requirements; an independent safety layer retains final authority.

  1. 01
    State uncertainty · anchored belief Condition the current physical state on measurements

    The observer maintains a distribution over the present state rather than one latent point. New telemetry updates hidden velocity, momentum, and contact uncertainty.

  2. 02
    Physical process uncertainty · structured noise Constrain physical stochasticity to dissipative channels

    The stochastic pH component models physical variability through the dissipation operator. Separately, model residuals track evidence that the deployed twin or its calibration is wrong.

  3. 03
    Candidate uncertainty · matched futures Roll every candidate through the same belief

    Each state or model sample produces one possible typed future. The candidate set and sample budget remain identical across predictive baselines.

  4. 04
    Held-out calibration · consequence bounds Calibrate the uncertainty of each typed consequence

    Coverage is evaluated separately by consequence, forecast horizon, and deployment condition. Narrow intervals are useful only when their empirical coverage remains valid.

  5. 05
    Tail-aware selection · downstream risk layer Optimize the bad tail, subject to calibrated envelopes

    The selector favors low tail cost rather than only the mean. Every safety-critical consequence must also satisfy its declared chance constraint.

  6. 06
    Bounded authority · hardware action Execute, re-observe, or fall back

    If a candidate passes, the human-approved pilot sends its intended descriptor to Spot's native controller. If uncertainty is reducible by sensing, the system pauses and refreshes. Otherwise it holds under the independent safety layer.

Calibration metrics

Are the probabilities honest?

Per-channel and per-horizon coverage, interval width, negative log-likelihood, CRPS, reliability under held-out payload/sensing conditions, and calibration drift.

Decision metrics

Does uncertainty improve choices?

False-safe and false-abstain rates, chance-constraint violations, tail cost, CVaR regret, task progress, energy, intervention frequency, and oracle regret.

Deployment metrics

Can the interface run responsibly?

Planning latency, stale-belief duration, re-observation frequency, shield interventions, emergency stops, operator overrides, and hard-limit margins.

Claim boundary: calibrated coverage is conditional on the calibration protocol and its deployment assumptions. A bounded pilot validates the decision interface, timing, and independent fallback path; a small number of harmless trials does not prove universal hardware safety.

C-PHAST Summer Progress

Over four months, C-PHAST progressed from an evidence-ready compositional architecture to a rigorously scoped world model with cross-robot transfer, real Spot forecasting, simulator-executed decision influence, and a reproducible public research package.

Summer arc

What changed?

The project began the summer with reusable pH blocks and several promising experiments. The work since then connected those pieces into one inspectable contract: compose the deployed system, forecast candidate futures, expose typed consequences, and let a downstream objective use them without hiding the physics.

Completed and scheduled work

Seven milestones

The first six milestones record completed evidence. The seventh makes the public manuscript release an explicit, dated deliverable.

  1. May 1-14Foundation

    Architecture became an evidence system

    Unified deployment specifications, coordinate charts, typed ports, reusable blocks, power-preserving interconnections, energy ledgers, residual reports, and candidate-future queries.

    • Completed 756 of 756 Go2 branch rollouts.
    • Logged 53,872 typed candidate-factor rows.
    • Localized microgrid changes through line-current, energy, and interconnection residuals.

    Outcome: the bottleneck moved from representation to collecting claim-matched evidence.

    See the composition contract
  2. May 19-23Real robot

    Spot telemetry became typed future consequences

    Audited Spot-with-arm ROS bags and built the real-data query from recent telemetry plus numeric candidate descriptors to work, support, sensing, collision, and novelty forecasts.

    • Six usable episodes and 1,070 five-second windows.
    • 897 training and 173 held-out windows.
    • Mean Pearson/Spearman objective agreement: 0.880/0.860.

    Outcome: typed predictions preserved scalar ranking quality while explaining why a card scored well or poorly.

    See the Spot evidence
  3. May 28-Jun 18Submission

    Separate results became one CoRL contract

    Assembled the CoRL submission around one information flow: observed history and deployment, candidate rollout, typed consequence readouts, planner factors, and downstream objectives.

    • Separated pH-native and learned consequence channels.
    • Clarified Spot shadow mode and the controller boundary.
    • Refactored benchmark registration and experiment organization.

    Outcome: cross-robot transfer, microgrid reconfiguration, push warnings, and Spot action cards were presented as one model interface.

  4. Jul 7-Aug 1Rigor

    Theory and transfer results were hardened

    Reworked the mathematical claims around finite-step energy accounting, conditional passivity, observer-aware transfer, deployment contraction, and numerical coupling.

    • Added the finite-step energy-balance defect analysis.
    • Added certified contraction and probabilistic transfer results.
    • Regenerated the canonical ANYmal-D to Go2 comparison.

    Result: Go2 H=50 MSE is 0.063 for C-PHAST, versus 0.087 for monolithic PHAST and 0.163 for the larger Shared-GRU.

    See the transfer result
  5. Aug 8-10Executed decision

    Forecasts were connected to executed commands

    Reviewer-driven experiments connected a frozen C-PHAST predictor to a high-level recovery selector while preserving the frozen low-level locomotion policy.

    • Diagnosed weak command-direction sensitivity.
    • Introduced causal actuator-to-torque port translation.
    • Evaluated nine commands on 20 held-out scenarios.

    Result: mean survival rose from 116.6 to 121.8 steps; pH readouts increased longest-survival selections from 12/20 to 14/20.

    Boundary: binary recovery remained 10/20 for both methods, so this is not recovery-rate dominance.

Workstream 01 Reconfiguration benchmark Can reusable C-PHAST blocks predict and explain failures after the system changes? Open study

Why this matters

C-PHAST already transfers across robot layouts and localizes microgrid changes. We have not yet tested prospective failure attribution across a broad held-out reconfiguration suite.

What we will do

Build matched before-and-after cases that change subsystem count, payload, contact, terrain, sensing, DGU membership, feeder topology, or load one intervention at a time.

What success means

Accurate held-out rollouts and correct identification of the dominant physical failure channel after reconfiguration.

Full experiment design
Matched contractHold the initial state, supplied inputs, and evaluation horizon fixed; change one declared deployment property and keep the altered case held out from training.
EvidenceCompare with monolithic PHAST, black-box sequence models, and appropriate graph or structured baselines using rollout error, power consistency, and residual localization.
Workstream 02 Counterfactual action benchmark From the same disturbed state, can C-PHAST correctly rank alternative recovery commands? Open study

Why this matters

The one-shot result shows that typed forecasts can influence selection, while the binary-recovery tie and exploratory planning expose limited sensitivity to command direction.

What we will do

Execute multiple command continuations from matched post-disturbance states, train on their differentiated consequences, and evaluate ranking before introducing repeated control.

What success means

Lower held-out action-ranking regret than state-only, kinematics-only, action-identity, and black-box prediction baselines.

Full experiment design
Matched contractUse the same observed state, push, terrain, low-level policy, command library, and horizon. Only the candidate command changes across branches.
EvidenceReport top-action accuracy, survival regret, pairwise ranking, consequence error, and the added value of pH readouts over predicted kinematics.
Workstream 03 Closed-loop controller study Do C-PHAST forecasts improve repeated recovery decisions beyond alarms and fixed responses? Open study

Why this matters

The current experiment makes one feedback decision: C-PHAST selects once and the frozen locomotion policy executes for 24 steps. It does not yet re-observe and replan.

What we will do

Freeze the action-conditioned model, repeat the one-shot test at scale, then evaluate receding-horizon discrete MPC and MPPI after the ranking gate passes.

What success means

Beat the best fixed recovery and black-box planner on held-out disturbances without increasing harms, work, tilt, or loss of task progress.

Full experiment design
ComparisonsNominal, alarm plus fixed recovery, best fixed command, state-only selection, black-box model planning, PHAST, C-PHAST without pH readouts, full C-PHAST, and an outcome oracle.
MeasurementsRecovery, survival, progress, mechanical work, tilt, rescues, harms, switching behavior, command regret, and planning latency under matched seeds and disturbances.
Workstream 04 Structure-aware controller study When can the pH representation support controller synthesis with explicit guarantees? Open study

Why this matters

Exact model-level power accounting provides a controller interface, but it is not by itself a stability theorem for a learned closed loop.

What we will do

Derive and test passivity-based microgrid control under declared plant and observation assumptions, using Energy-Casimir design only where matching conditions can be established.

What success means

State the guarantee and region of validity precisely, then demonstrate regulation and transient behavior consistent with those assumptions.

Full experiment design
ComparisonsNominal or uncontrolled dynamics, an established domain controller, and a learned model-based alternative under the same disturbances and reconfigurations.
MeasurementsRegulation error, transient energy, supplied and dissipated power, balance residuals, stability conditions, and controller sensitivity to model mismatch.
Workstream 05 Stochastic C-PHAST study Can calibrated consequence uncertainty improve risk-sensitive decisions? Open study

Why this matters

Point forecasts can rank expected outcomes but cannot distinguish a consistently safe command from one with the same mean and a dangerous tail.

What we will do

Learn calibrated distributions over typed consequences and use them for chance constraints, tail-risk ranking, and risk-sensitive closed-loop planning.

What success means

Improve held-out tail risk and safety outcomes while reporting calibration and nominal performance rather than hiding the tradeoff.

Full experiment design
ComparisonsDeterministic C-PHAST, empirical or ensemble uncertainty, stochastic C-PHAST, and an outcome-distribution oracle where simulator branches permit it.
MeasurementsCalibration, coverage, proper scoring rules, tail-risk ranking, chance-constraint violations, recovery outcomes, and nominal efficiency.
Cross-cutting deliverable

Reproducibility, matched replay, and hardware progression

Release code, checkpoints, configurations, splits, scripts, and result manifests. An interactive replay will compare methods under the same state, seed, disturbance, terrain, and low-level policy while marking each view as completed evidence, saved replay, or planned work. Bounded Spot execution begins only after the frozen planner passes the held-out simulator gate.

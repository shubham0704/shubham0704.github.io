C-PHAST Summer Progress

Over four months, C-PHAST progressed from an evidence-ready compositional architecture to a rigorously scoped world model with cross-robot transfer, real Spot forecasting, simulator-executed decision influence, and a reproducible public research package.

Summer arc

What changed?

The project began the summer with reusable pH blocks and several promising experiments. The work since then connected those pieces into one inspectable contract: compose the deployed system, forecast candidate futures, expose typed consequences, and let a downstream objective use them without hiding the physics.

Completed work

Six milestones

Each milestone records the scientific change, a concrete result, and the claim boundary retained in the paper.

  1. May 1-14Foundation

    Architecture became an evidence system

    Unified deployment specifications, coordinate charts, typed ports, reusable blocks, power-preserving interconnections, energy ledgers, residual reports, and candidate-future queries.

    • Completed 756 of 756 Go2 branch rollouts.
    • Logged 53,872 typed candidate-factor rows.
    • Localized microgrid changes through line-current, energy, and interconnection residuals.

    Outcome: the bottleneck moved from representation to collecting claim-matched evidence.

    See the composition contract
  2. May 19-23Real robot

    Spot telemetry became typed future consequences

    Audited Spot-with-arm ROS bags and built the real-data query from recent telemetry plus numeric candidate descriptors to work, support, sensing, collision, and novelty forecasts.

    • Six usable episodes and 1,070 five-second windows.
    • 897 training and 173 held-out windows.
    • Mean Pearson/Spearman objective agreement: 0.880/0.860.

    Outcome: typed predictions preserved scalar ranking quality while explaining why a card scored well or poorly.

    See the Spot evidence
  3. May 28-Jun 18Submission

    Separate results became one CoRL contract

    Assembled the CoRL submission around one information flow: observed history and deployment, candidate rollout, typed consequence readouts, planner factors, and downstream objectives.

    • Separated pH-native and learned consequence channels.
    • Clarified Spot shadow mode and the controller boundary.
    • Refactored benchmark registration and experiment organization.

    Outcome: cross-robot transfer, microgrid reconfiguration, push warnings, and Spot action cards were presented as one model interface.

    Open the full manuscript
  4. Jul 7-Aug 1Rigor

    Theory and transfer results were hardened

    Reworked the mathematical claims around finite-step energy accounting, conditional passivity, observer-aware transfer, deployment contraction, and numerical coupling.

    • Added the finite-step energy-balance defect analysis.
    • Added certified contraction and probabilistic transfer results.
    • Regenerated the canonical ANYmal-D to Go2 comparison.

    Result: Go2 H=50 MSE is 0.063 for C-PHAST, versus 0.087 for monolithic PHAST and 0.163 for the larger Shared-GRU.

    See the transfer result
  5. Aug 8-10Executed decision

    Forecasts were connected to executed commands

    Reviewer-driven experiments connected a frozen C-PHAST predictor to a high-level recovery selector while preserving the frozen low-level locomotion policy.

    • Diagnosed weak command-direction sensitivity.
    • Introduced causal actuator-to-torque port translation.
    • Evaluated nine commands on 20 held-out scenarios.

    Result: mean survival rose from 116.6 to 121.8 steps; pH readouts increased longest-survival selections from 12/20 to 14/20.

    Boundary: binary recovery remained 10/20 for both methods, so this is not recovery-rate dominance.

    See the executed evidence
  6. Aug 14-17Communication

    The research package became inspectable

    Built live PHAST and C-PHAST posters that connect the papers, experiments, videos, equations, model contracts, and composition examples in one presentation surface.

    • Added subsystem-count and topology-change media.
    • Added exact model inputs, outputs, losses, and objective definitions.
    • Added interactive mathematical variable definitions.

    Outcome: the project can now be presented from motivation through formulation, evidence, limitations, and next steps without relying on hidden appendix context.

Next research phase

From reconfigurable forecasts to executed decisions

The next phase asks when C-PHAST remains reliable after a system changes, whether its counterfactual forecasts improve repeated decisions, and where the port-Hamiltonian structure can support controller design. Each stage has an evidence gate before hardware execution.

  1. 01Reconfiguration forecastingPredict changed systems and failure channels
  2. 02Action-conditioned predictionSeparate the consequences of candidate commands
  3. 03Simulator closed loopRe-observe, replan, and execute repeatedly
  4. 04Bounded hardware executionProceed only after held-out simulation gates
Workstream 01

Reconfiguration and failure-mode modeling

Scientific question Can reusable C-PHAST blocks remain valid after component, topology, contact, or sensing changes and identify the physical channel through which failure develops?

Planned work
Construct matched robotics and microgrid cases with changed subsystem counts, payloads, contacts, terrain, sensors, DGU membership, feeder topology, and loads.
Evaluation
Measure zero-shot rollout accuracy, energy and power consistency, typed residual localization, and counterfactual ranking against monolithic PHAST, black-box sequence models, and appropriate graph or structured baselines.
Completion gate
Demonstrate transfer to held-out reconfigurations without retraining while correctly attributing the dominant failure channel.
Workstream 02

Action-conditioned planning and control

Scientific question Can C-PHAST's typed counterfactual forecasts select better recovery commands than an alarm followed by a fixed response?

Planned work
Collect multiple command continuations from matched disturbed states. Extend the current one-shot selector first to receding-horizon discrete MPC, then to MPPI after command-ranking accuracy is established.
Evaluation
Compare nominal continuation, alarm-plus-fixed recovery, the best fixed command, state-only selection, black-box world-model planning, PHAST, C-PHAST without pH readouts, full C-PHAST, and an outcome oracle. Report recovery, survival, progress, work, tilt, rescues, harms, and planning latency.
Completion gate
Outperform the best fixed response and black-box planner on held-out disturbances without sacrificing task progress or energy use.
Workstream 03

Structure-aware and uncertainty-aware control

Scientific question When does the port-Hamiltonian representation provide more than forecast accuracy: a useful controller interface, interpretable risk, or a verifiable stability argument?

Planned work
Derive and test passivity-based microgrid control, using Energy-Casimir design only where its matching conditions can be established. Extend stochastic C-PHAST to calibrated consequence distributions for risk-sensitive planning.
Evaluation
Report regulation, transient energy, power-balance residuals, and stability conditions for the microgrid study; report calibration, coverage, tail-risk ranking, and closed-loop outcomes for stochastic planning.
Completion gate
State every control guarantee with its assumptions and show that uncertainty-aware decisions improve held-out risk without hiding nominal performance.
Cross-cutting deliverable

Reproducibility and hardware progression

Release the C-PHAST code, checkpoints, configurations, splits, scripts, and result manifests, including a clear record of exploratory and final controller experiments. Bounded Spot execution begins only after the frozen planner passes the held-out simulator gate.

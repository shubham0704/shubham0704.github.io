ABX3 adaptive material discovery · research progress

Can a learned policy find novel materials with target properties at lower first-principles cost?

We study a discovery policy that learns where to look. A recurrent HNet/PNet controller searches a 643-dimensional continuous space of five-site chemistry and lattice geometry, updates itself between episodes from live PBEsol-relax/TB09 measurements, and reuses audited observations as navigation anchors. Two co-primary tests measure it: the first-principles compute needed to reach a withheld target property, and the number of novel validated candidates returned when no target is given. Both claims remain contingent on completed live-DFT evidence.

Research questions

Three questions define the study.

Q1 and Q2 each have a registered experiment and failure condition. Q3 does not yet: no arm in the current matrix varies response supervision. See Frontier 02.

  1. Q1 · NavigationDoes the policy improve over episodes?

    Measure DFT compute to a withheld oracle-consistent target.

  2. Q2 · DiscoveryDoes it find better unknown candidates?

    Measure unique, feasible, independently validated ABX3 yield.

  3. Q3 · Physical supervisionDo first-principles responses improve learning?

    Test PBEsol structural and family-constrained APDFT energy tangents.

03 · Co-primary empirical program

Two empirical tracks under live DFT

Target recovery tests navigation. Open-ended search tests discovery.

Track A · Known-target recovery: oracle calls and core-hours to a withheld target.Track B · Open-ended material discovery: unique validated yield without a reference structure. Both policies remain adaptive across measured episodes.

Decision rule. Support requires a stable paired advantage after charging failures, retries, and derivative costs; the memory claim additionally requires an advantage over the no-memory arm. A null result is no stable paired advantage, no episode-wise learning trend, or no memory benefit.

04 · Open frontiers

What is not yet known

Each frontier states what is demonstrated, what must be built, and the result that would close it. A frontier can fail its own gate.

Frontier 01open

Can a differentiable search policy stay inside the domain its surrogate was calibrated on?

Move from clipping out-of-domain proposals after the fact toward dynamics that never leave the calibrated region.

Open target
The geometry scored by the acquisition function is the geometry evaluated by the oracle.
Current evidence
Across all completed v75 HNet runs, 51.5 to 70 percent of charged candidates were proposed outside the compiled domain of 5.2 to 6.8 A and clipped before evaluation. The p95 excursion exceeds one domain width and the maximum reaches 4.99. Charged evaluations themselves stayed in-domain: 0 of 1950 accepted candidates violated the bound.
Research advance required
Replace the unbounded log-lattice chart and its soft clip penalty with a smooth bounded parameterization or principled constrained dynamics, so proposals are admissible by construction rather than by projection.
Completion gate
On a re-run matrix, clip distance collapses to approximately zero at p95 while recovery efficiency is no worse than the unconstrained policy under matched charged-call and core-hour accounting.
Established problem

Constrained and manifold-aware optimization, trust-region methods, and reparameterized bounded search are standard. Surrogate-model literature likewise warns against querying outside the calibration envelope.

Our question goes further. The question here is narrower and sharper: when a learned potential is differentiated through a physical relaxation surrogate, does enforcing the domain constraint inside the dynamics preserve the search efficiency that the unconstrained relaxation appeared to buy?

Evidence boundary. The excursion statistics and the gradient blow-up are measured. The component that originates the blow-up is not yet isolated, and the rollout-interior excursion rate is measured for one instrumented run only.

Frontier 02unregistered

Does measured first-principles response actually improve the learned policy?

Move from verifying that physical tangents are numerically correct toward showing that they change search behaviour.

Open target
A stable paired advantage attributable to response supervision rather than to the rest of the loop.
Current evidence
Three representative family directions passed centered-response checks with Richardson errors no larger than 5.99 x 10^-7 eV per simplex coordinate. That establishes the supervision signal is numerically sound.
Research advance required
Register a response-supervision ablation. The current matrix cannot answer this question: both HNet arms enable stability and energy supervision identically and differ only in evaluated-waypoint restarts, so no contrast isolates the response term.
Completion gate
A sixth arm identical to the proposed method with response supervision disabled shows a reproducible paired difference in core-hours to first hit, or the question is retired and the claim withdrawn.
Established problem

Derivative-informed surrogates and gradient-enhanced regression report sample-efficiency gains, generally under analytic or cheaply available derivatives.

Our question goes further. Here the derivative is an expensive, family-constrained first-principles response supplied as detached supervision across a stop-gradient measurement boundary. Whether that buys search efficiency at live-DFT cost is untested.

Evidence boundary. Numerical validity of the tangents is demonstrated. Their effect on discovery performance is entirely unmeasured, and no registered experiment currently varies them.

Frontier 03registry pending

Does the policy discover useful candidates when no target is given?

Move from recovering a withheld target toward yielding unique, feasible, independently validated materials.

Open target
Unique validated ABX3 yield at matched charged-call and core-hour budgets, with no hidden target structure.
Current evidence
The closed feedback loop, the shared oracle contract, and the matched accounting interfaces are built and exercised by the known-target track. No open-ended run has been executed.
Research advance required
Freeze the open-ended registry: candidate family and parameterization, property and stability objectives, feasibility, novelty and independent-validation gates, budgets, seeds, exact baseline behaviour, and the analysis contract.
Completion gate
Every method searches the registered domain under one accounting contract, and shortlisted candidates survive independent PBEsol-relax and TB09 reruns.
Established problem

Generative and active-learning pipelines for inorganic materials commonly report screened candidate counts, typically against fixed surrogates rather than a live first-principles oracle inside the loop.

Our question goes further. The question is whether a policy that updates from measured physics between episodes returns a better validated set than matched baselines at equal physical cost, rather than a larger unvalidated shortlist.

Evidence boundary. This is a co-primary manuscript claim with no evidence yet. It is not a future-work extension of the known-target result, and the known-target analyzer must not be adapted post hoc to stand in for it.

\paragraph{Empirical grounding of the probabilistic transfer bound.}
We evaluate Theorem~\ref{thm:prob-transfer} on the affine damped oscillator with
$k=2$, $d=3$, and step size $0.05$. The numerical metric used for this coverage
study is distinct from the exact witness in
Remark~\ref{rem:certified-linear-contraction}; both use the same reported
contraction factor. A stationary-gain, q-only Kalman
observer uses $Q_\theta=10^{-6}I$ and $R=I$. Writing its gain as $K$ and
position-observation matrix as $C$, the signed error evolves through
\[
 A_e=(I-KC)A,\qquad
 \Sigma=(I-KC)Q_\theta(I-KC)^\top+KRK^\top.
\]
The implementation checks $\|A_e\|_M\le q$ before applying the recursion.
Initialization is an independent draw from $\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)$, as are
the subsequent injections; it is not drawn from the stationary posterior
covariance. We use $N=3000$ independent rollouts of horizon $h=200$.
The table reports coverage of
$\max_{0\leq j\leq h}e_j$ and the ratio of the bound to its empirical
$(1-\alpha)$-quantile.  The C.5-max columns use the simultaneous-max extension
of Remark~\ref{rem:prob-transfer-concentration}, including both its
$\sqrt{\log(h+1)}$ and $\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}$ terms. Every measured coverage was one,
above the required Monte-Carlo lower band, but this is an empirical check and
not a certificate.  The C.4 envelope is conservative: its ratio is
$17$--$18\times$ on the oscillator.  Independent-refresh concentration is tighter,
reducing this range to $6$--$7\times$, while retaining
the same measured coverage; neither result supports a claim of tightness.
